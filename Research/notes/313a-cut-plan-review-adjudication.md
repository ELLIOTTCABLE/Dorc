## § 1-the-verdict

The plan's structure survived all three reviews: cutting by product question, checking one rule
at a time against given answers, and the induction over derivations. All three reviewers
examined the induction and found it valid for well-founded derivations.

Most of what the plan argues beyond that did not survive as written. Of its seven arguments,
five claim more than they show. Two findings change the design work and not only the wording:
separation is licensed by no statement that exists today, and the one-instant argument omits two
premises. No finding was judged manufactured. Two restate limits the plan's author already held
(§ 4).

## § 2-the-findings

Findings that agree are grouped. A finding raised only under adversarial framing is marked
*adversarial only*; each of those was re-derived before being credited.

### § 2.1-separation-needs-speech-that-does-not-exist

`313d:fnd-separation-is-a-held-hole-not-todays-speech`; `313b:preservation-needs-more-than-soundness`.

- Plan's sentence: "the statements an author must make are the same ones; only the document that
  reads them changes."
- HOLDS. *Read* and *argued*; the counterexample *reviewer ran*. A unique-name statement yields
  two things, never two things with no common part. The existing specification holds exactly
  this case open (`hole_two_separated_things_reach_one_thing_beneath`) and asks its sparing law
  outside it. Defining the negative answer as separation does not close that hole; it names it.
- The neutral review adds the general form: soundness says nothing about how many answers
  survive the cut, and one floor witness excludes only losing all of them. The plan claimed to
  answer `313:risk-the-factoring-forces-more-guarding` and argued only soundness.
- Consequence: either a new statement by a store's describer that its members share no part, or
  the sibling rule is withdrawn and more is guarded. This is the plan's largest unpaid cost.

### § 2.2-the-one-instant-argument-omits-two-premises

`313d:fnd-the-instant-seam-forgets-the-writers-names`; `313c:survival-does-not-frame-the-vantage`.
Both *adversarial only*.

- Plan's sentence: "If every write between the instant and the use is separate from all of
  those, then ... each name still denotes the same thing in the same state."
- HOLDS, both. *Argued*; the first also *reviewer ran* on a variant of the plan's own rename
  model.
- The writer's names. The argument carries only the fact's names forward. A writing line's names
  were also resolved at the probe, and an earlier line can re-point them onto the fact's thing
  while touching nothing the fact depends on. The existing specification does guard both sides
  (`compareAt` reads staleness of both keys); the plan dropped that.
- The vantage. The plan's own axiom makes a name's meaning depend on the route and on the
  vantage, and then preserves only the route. A `cd` between measurement and use changes what a
  relative name means with no host state changed.
- The same review shows that the plan's rename model was green only because its uniqueness
  warrant was true at every instant, which is a promise of persistence the plan says nothing
  static makes.

### § 2.3-the-flag-class-has-no-fixed-extension

`313d:fnd-the-bounded-class-moves-with-what-is-called-named`;
`313b:local-greens-do-not-preclude-late-reds`.

- Plan's sentences: the definition of *bounded*, and "Checked in a scratch model".
- HOLDS for two legs, OPEN for a third. *Argued*; two legs *reviewer ran*.
- The scratch model froze a sort's membership across its two worlds. With membership free, the
  sort-scoped negative is not local to what it names. "Checked" overstated what was checked.
- The class is decided by what a statement names, which its author writes. A sort that holds
  everything gives a sort-scoped negative the meaning of "nothing but these" while leaving it
  classed as bounded. That is gating by statement kind through another door.
- The neutral review's form of the same point: the three corollaries hold for any choice of
  class, so their being green says nothing about whether the class is the right one.
- OPEN: whether a fact's dependence is itself a closure. The existing specification makes it
  speech ("reads nothing but these"); `30U` calls a backing constitutive of its fact. Under the
  first reading no flag-free floor exists at all. This needs a ruling before the floor witness
  means anything.

### § 2.4-adding-vocabulary-can-change-a-lower-result

`313b:new-signatures-can-change-old-answers`; `313c:signature-extension-can-change-lower-results`.

- Plan's sentence: "An upper file can only add world-shapes, definitions, or facts. The first
  two cannot change a lower answer."
- HOLDS. *Argued* from the language's definition of an abstract signature; *reviewer ran*. A
  lower formula that mentions an abstract parent changes truth when an upper file adds a child.
  The plan's core puts every statement kind under one abstract parent, so this is its own shape.
- Consequence: re-running a lower document's witnesses from the top is not enough, and scoping
  the new signature to zero tests only the old worlds. Each document's checks have to be asked
  again in the assembled universe.

### § 2.5-true-speech-can-arm-a-false-statement

`313b:true-additions-can-activate-falsehood`; `313d:fnd-true-speech-arms-a-loaded-false-statement`.

- Plan's sentences: "loading true speech never makes a right answer wrong"; "That world is the
  false-closure cell, and it is flag-class."
- HOLDS. *Argued*; *reviewer ran*. A true statement can complete a derivation whose other
  premise was already loaded and false. The false premise need not be a closure, so the world is
  not confined to the flag class. What survives is attribution: the false statement is in the
  chain.

### § 2.6-the-naming-and-extent-seam-is-not-the-razor

`313c:naming-warrants-are-not-all-positive`. *Adversarial only.*

- Plan's sentence: "The seam between naming and extent is the razor's own seam: positive,
  line-sayable speech on one side, closures on the other."
- HOLDS. *Argued* on the plan's own rule model. "Inside one container a spelling names one
  thing" is false after one more name is placed in the container, so it is not positive by the
  plan's own definition. Producing *same*, being line-sayable, and being positive are three
  different properties.

### § 2.7-overlap-is-not-exclusive-of-same

`313d:fnd-overlap-is-not-exclusive-or-not-positive`. *Adversarial only.*

- Plan's sentences: "Overlap is a positive answer"; "under true speech old answers stand".
- HOLDS. *Argued*. As defined, a same pair is an overlap pair, so two definite answers are right
  at once and a sound new rule moves a recorded overlap to same. Narrowed to exclude same,
  overlap asserts difference and needs a negative statement.

### § 2.8-every-new-answer-re-founds-the-core

`313d:fnd-every-answers-meaning-lands-in-the-core`. *Adversarial only.*

- Plan's sentence: "Adding a rule document changes no other document's text or key."
- HOLDS IN PART. *Argued*. True for a rule over answers the core already defines. False for the
  effect and order documents, which add answers whose meaning must sit in the core because other
  documents take them as given. Each re-keys every row, and the hard nouns pool in the core, so
  "documents of comparable size" is doubtful.

### § 2.9-revocation-has-no-law

`313b:truth-does-not-establish-current-consent`.

- HOLDS, small. *Argued*. The theorem is satisfied by an answer kept after its closure was
  withdrawn. The missing law is one line: an answer's chain lies inside the speech it was
  computed from.

### § 2.10-the-assembly-is-unmeasured-and-its-fixpoint-unstated

`313d:fnd-the-whole-design-tier-has-no-measured-path`. *Adversarial only*; the reviewer's own
confidence is low.

- HOLDS IN PART. *Argued*. That the assembly is unmeasured and needs a missing feature is a
  limit the author already held (§ 4). The new part stands: the plan does not say how the
  assembled engine is encoded, and a table that is merely closed under the rules admits answers
  that support each other in a circle, which the induction does not cover.
