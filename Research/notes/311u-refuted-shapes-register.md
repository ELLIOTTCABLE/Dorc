# 311u — Refuted shapes: the register

> AI-authored (Fable). Notes-tier and ahistorical. A sibling of `notes/311`, minted at the
> human's direction as a reference for this phase of design. Nothing here is ruled. Each entry
> records a shape the model does not take and the case that decided it. An entry leaves this
> register when a later case overturns it. The register carries no dates and no sitting references.
> Cite an entry as `311u:slug`. Section slugs are 311's own. A `Research/GOTCHAS.md` slug appears
> in parentheses where a case is one.

## Conventions

- "Shape" means a candidate rule or object the model once considered.
- "Case" means the situation that refuted the shape.
- "Survivor" means what 311 says instead.
- The register is not exhaustive. It keeps the shapes a reader of 311 is most likely to reach
  for again.

## § 1-species-and-objects

### refuted-one-declared-species

**Shape.** An MSort is itself a way of writing, and a second way of writing is a second MSort.
**Case.** The model then writes every MSort-level declaration once per way of writing. The
may-read set, the entailment, the finished record, the observer-dependence, and the cells are all
such declarations. One author writes every copy, and no seat says that the copies describe one
thing. Two finished records for one carrier land as a withhold, not as the contradiction they
are. A stranger who holds only a new lookup must mint a whole MSort or leave it empty.
**Survivor.** Two species, MSort and MScheme (1.2-sort-the-declared-carrier,
1.3-scheme-a-way-of-writing).

### refuted-sort-as-a-world-category

**Shape.** An MSort is a category of the world, the thing meant.
**Case.** The engine never holds an MReferent, and strangers mint overlapping vocabularies
constantly (`one-state-reached-through-two-kinds`). A world category makes the disjointness of
two MSorts an assumption, and that assumption is a wrong DISJOINT.
**Survivor.** An MSort is a declared carrier. A cross-MSort pair reads KNOWN_UNSPOKEN unless
the walk separates it (1.2-sort-the-declared-carrier, 3.2-compare-one-chokepoint-four-answers).

### refuted-default-scheme-per-sort

**Shape.** Each MSort has a default MScheme, so a bind may name the MSort alone.
**Case.** The MSort then stands where an MScheme belongs, and the bind stops saying which lookup
the author holds.
**Survivor.** A bind or a mark always names an MScheme (1.3-scheme-a-way-of-writing).

### refuted-two-parent-edges-per-key

**Shape.** An MKey has two parents, an MParent-Catalog and an MParent-Store, as two edges.
**Case.** At the primary MScheme the two are one MKey. A secondary MScheme's MKey has no
identity of its own, only the identity of its yield.
**Survivor.** One MParent per MKey, and the MScheme decides what the edge carries
(1.6-parent-one-per-key).

### refuted-one-parent-sort-per-scheme

**Shape.** A primary MScheme declares one MParent MSort for all of its MKeys.
**Case.** The MParent of a filesystem identifier varies with the filesystem's type. An ext4
filesystem sits in the MRoute, an NFS filesystem in a host, and a tmpfs in a boot. An sshfs
mints inode numbers per client. The child MSort and its MSchemes do not vary with any of that.
**Survivor.** The owner declares the MParent MSort and the warrants per matched shape of the
MValue (2.2-primary-of-and-identified-in).

### refuted-subsorts-or-a-type-menu

**Shape.** Subsorts, a menu of types, or one member per type carry the variation of
`refuted-one-parent-sort-per-scheme`.
**Case.** A second name is a second MScheme, and the variation is a partial function of one
MScheme's MValues.
**Survivor.** Per-shape declarations on one `resolve()` (2.2-primary-of-and-identified-in).

### refuted-deriving-the-store-warrant-from-chain-shape

**Shape.** The engine derives `:aliases-nothing-else` from the shape of the MFullyQualifiedKey.
**Case.** Nested pid namespaces (`correspondence-is-known-only-to-the-transition-owner`). Guest
pid 1 and host pid 4821 are one MReferent, and both chains resolve cleanly to one boot.
**Survivor.** The store's describer declares the warrant, per store. It is absent by default
(2.3-aliases-nothing-else-the-store-warrant).

### refuted-an-aspect-species

**Shape.** A selector position, or an aspect that borrows another MSort's lookup, is a species
of its own.
**Case.** One MParent per identity-bearing thing, a per-aspect may-read set, and a per-aspect
observer-dependence already make an aspect an MSort in all but name. The borrowed lookup is the
MParent's primary MScheme, and the aspect is `:identified-in` the MParent's MSort.
**Survivor.** A cell is a singleton MSort under its MParent (1.9-cell-a-singleton-sort).

### refuted-a-term-for-parts

**Shape.** A term, such as "field", names the parts of an MReferent.
**Case.** Every part is an ordinary MReferent of an ordinary MSort. A term over a sorted,
tracked MReferent privileges one graph of MSort inhabitants over the others.
**Survivor.** 1.1-referent-state-and-value and 1.9-cell-a-singleton-sort.

### refuted-a-question-species

**Shape.** A species beside the MReferent names what a read asks: a property, an expression, or
a claim.
**Case.** The engine cannot tell a stored singleton from a derived one, and it does not need to.
The marked line is a read of an MKey, and the fact's identity is its MTopic.
**Survivor.** 1.8-fully-qualified-key-topic-and-derivation and 1.9-cell-a-singleton-sort.

## § 2-identity-and-the-walk

### refuted-identity-as-a-table-against-axes

**Shape.** Identity is a per-MSort table against context axes: invariant, keyed, or ⊤ per
index-kind, with a filtered meet over the axes.
**Case.** The MSort owner cannot know the axes. Silence walls forever. A guess such as "keyed by
Host", with a referent-transparent Host, spares a fact on a shared volume
(`a-host-is-not-a-partition`).
**Survivor.** A cell's identity is a property of what its MKey denotes, the MFullyQualifiedKey,
and not of the MScheme that the author wrote the MKey in
(1.8-fully-qualified-key-topic-and-derivation, 3.3-invalidation-three-mutator-species).

### refuted-context-inside-the-key

**Shape.** The context is part of a fact's key.
**Case.** A qualified MKey is the address of a question and asserts no partition. The dangerous
inference lived in the meet's generators, not in the key.
**Survivor.** The MVantage is an address, and it keys the `witness()`
(1.10-vantage-route-placeholder-witness).

### refuted-vantage-root-as-a-machine

**Shape.** The MRoute is the machine: a store with children and a may-read set of its own.
**Case.** A view over NFS, a tunnel, and a guest whose disk is a file on a host. The place a
probe stood holds no state, and "the machine" names several things that boot, mount, and clone
apart (`identity-tokens-have-clone-horizons`, `a-host-is-not-a-partition`).
**Survivor.** The MRoute is an address with no may-read set, and a chain that ends there yields
no survival. A boot, a volume, and an instance are `:root` shapes that their issuers' describers
declare, each with its horizon (1.10-vantage-route-placeholder-witness,
2.2-primary-of-and-identified-in).

### refuted-terminal-tokens

**Shape.** A `resolve()` returns a terminal token, such as a filesystem id with an inode, and the
engine compares tokens.
**Case.** NFS, and the MParent question. An inode is an MKey in a filesystem, and a filesystem
identifier is an MKey in whatever minted it. The File owner never learns NFS, because the
MParent MSort's primary MScheme classifies one level up.
**Survivor.** `:root` as an explicit claim, and every MToken scoped in an MParent
(1.5-token-and-the-two-warrants, 2.2-primary-of-and-identified-in).

### refuted-one-grade-per-lookup

**Shape.** One grade on a lookup: transparent or identifying.
**Case.** Clones (`identity-tokens-have-clone-horizons`). Equal machine-ids on two machines
threaten a wrong SAME, and a grade that speaks of disjointness cannot protect against it.
**Survivor.** Two independent warrants per lookup, `:guarantees-unique-referent` and
`:guarantees-unique-name` (1.5-token-and-the-two-warrants).

### refuted-equal-keys-reach-one-referent-by-default

**Shape.** Equal MKeys in one MParent-Catalog across an unwalled span reach one MReferent,
because that is what an MScheme is.
**Case.** The default feeds only the dangerous consumer, since kill-reach already collides on
UNKNOWN. Recreation under a stable name, pid reuse, round-robin lookups, cached lookups, and a
moving `latest` tag are ordinary ops (`a-recreated-name-is-a-new-referent`,
`recycled-keys-outrun-the-unwalled-span`, `resolution-is-set-valued`,
`a-cached-lookup-answers-for-the-past`). The claim's owner differs at every level.
**Survivor.** The engine vouches the local MRoute under no wrapper, and the ambient and
inherited instances in one span. A lookup's functionality is its owner's opt-in
(1.5-token-and-the-two-warrants, 1.10-vantage-route-placeholder-witness).

### refuted-the-disclosed-weak-name-floor

**Shape.** Within one MSort, unequal names answer DISJOINT, and the rule discloses the answer as
weak.
**Case.** A synonym pair, two names for one MReferent, spares past a real write
(`distinct-names-alias-within-a-kind`). The floor also needed a carve per MSort, which made the
rule an engine table.
**Survivor.** The owner declares `:guarantees-unique-name`. The engine assumes nothing
(1.5-token-and-the-two-warrants).

### refuted-shared-ancestors-as-collisions

**Shape.** Two MKeys collide when their chains share an ancestor.
**Case.** A package status file and a unit file share one filesystem. Every pair on a host then
collides at the boot.
**Survivor.** Ancestors are MParents, and `compare()` decides at the divergence
(3.2-compare-one-chokepoint-four-answers).

### refuted-route-versus-root-as-known-unspoken

**Shape.** A chain that ends at the MRoute against a chain that ends at a MRoot reads
KNOWN_UNSPOKEN, and so do two MRoots.
**Case.** The finished definition then spends across MWorlds. A chain with fewer measured links
compares more decisively than the same chain measured whole. The Package author becomes the
wrong name in the why chain.
**Survivor.** Both read UNKNOWN, and partial measurement never widens
(3.2-compare-one-chokepoint-four-answers).

### refuted-separation-from-two-definitions

**Shape.** The walk separates two MKeys by combining distinctions from two definitions.
**Case.** One definition's store sits inside a shared MParent, and another definition's MKey
sits directly in that MParent. One slot then has two vocabularies, every positive sentence is
true, and the pair reads DISJOINT.
**Survivor.** An omission is a distinction only inside the body that made it
(3.2-compare-one-chokepoint-four-answers).

### refuted-parent-partitions-its-children

**Shape.** The owner of a container declares that its children, of whatever MSort, are pairwise
distinct things.
**Case.** Children minted by other definitions are not in the sentence. The sentence either
covers them, which their owner never said, or leaves them to collide with everything.
**Survivor.** Both sides identify into one MScheme whose body warrants `:guarantees-unique-name`
(3.2-compare-one-chokepoint-four-answers). A stranger's thing spares by filing itself into that
MScheme.

### refuted-the-two-schemes-disagreement-canary

**Shape.** Two MSchemes of one MSort resolve one string, in one MParent, to two different
MKey-Primaries, and the engine withholds both.
**Case.** An MKey is an MValue with its MScheme and its MParent, so one string under two
MSchemes is two questions. `sudo -u 1000` against `sudo -u '#1000'` is one example, and a branch
and a tag both named `release` is another. A lookup without `:guarantees-unique-name` cannot
contradict anything by returning two MTokens. The rule refused two correct authors and named
both.
**Survivor.** The grade governs contradiction (2.2-primary-of-and-identified-in).

### refuted-measurement-replaces-declarations

**Shape.** Once a `resolve()` exists, the declarations it was meant to replace are redundant.
**Case.** A `resolve()` establishes an MToken. It does not establish the MToken's MParent, its
MTopic, its warrant, its applicability after a change, or its sufficiency for the claim consumed.
**Survivor.** Measurement relocates speech to questions the owner can answer, and the
declarations remain (1.5-token-and-the-two-warrants, 2.2-primary-of-and-identified-in,
2.8-observer-dependence-and-independence).

## § 3-stores-read-sets-and-write-sets

### refuted-store-sets-compared-as-bags

**Shape.** The places where a thing's state lives form a set, and two things compare by their
sets.
**Case.** A base and an overlay (`composite-identity-is-structure-not-a-bag`). The same set with
the roles swapped answers differently. Pairwise set equality is also not reflexive in the
intended sense.
**Survivor.** One MParent, and a MCompositeSort for roles (1.6-parent-one-per-key,
2.11-composite-sorts-and-roles).

### refuted-stored-in-as-one-relation

**Shape.** One relation, "stored in", carries both routing and containment.
**Case.** Hardlinks, bind mounts, and NFS (`address-inequality-is-not-referent-inequality`,
`containment-by-path-prefix-lies`). MParent-Catalog disjointness is not MReferent disjointness.
**Survivor.** Three relations: `:yields` with its MParent-Catalog, the store's
`:aliases-nothing-else`, and may-read (2.1-yields-into-another-scheme,
2.3-aliases-nothing-else-the-store-warrant, 2.5-may-read-the-readset).

### refuted-union-totality-with-no-author

**Shape.** An MSort-level may-read set and a MSite-level set close by one sentinel over their
union.
**Case.** Nobody closed the union.
**Survivor.** The owner declares and closes the set, and MSites fill MParent-Catalogs
(2.5-may-read-the-readset, 2.1-yields-into-another-scheme).

### refuted-identity-carries-every-observation

**Shape.** One MReferent gives one fact, whichever probe read it.
**Case.** `test -w` under two users on one file (`the-subject-includes-the-observer`).
**Survivor.** `:observer-dependence` per MSort, dependent by default
(2.8-observer-dependence-and-independence).

### refuted-closure-as-reachable-only-through-me

**Shape.** A store's warrant says that its contents are reachable only through it.
**Case.** Nobody can know what else aliases their store. An overlay, an export, or a bind mount
arrives later with no mark on the viewed side (`backing-is-not-presenting`).
**Survivor.** `:aliases-nothing-else` is self-knowledge, and the walk asks it of every store on
both legs (2.3-aliases-nothing-else-the-store-warrant,
3.2-compare-one-chokepoint-four-answers).

### refuted-finished-definition-generates-disjoint

**Shape.** A finished definition generates DISJOINT across MSorts.
**Case.** `ctr task kill` against a converged `docker start` of the same container. "Nothing
else" is no other thing, never no other MKey for the thing written.
**Survivor.** `compare()` decides every pair, and the record witnesses a complete write set
(2.6-may-write-the-writeset, 3.2-compare-one-chokepoint-four-answers).

### refuted-only-same-entries-overlap

**Shape.** Two entries of a write set and a read set overlap only when they are one place.
**Case.** A vendor's store under a shared MParent against a knob directly in that MParent. The
two places are unequal, nobody spoke, and the test spares the knob's fact. This is
`refuted-the-disclosed-weak-name-floor` moved onto may-read entries.
**Survivor.** Whatever is not DISJOINT collides (2.6-may-write-the-writeset).

### refuted-parent-as-an-implicit-read-entry

**Shape.** An MKey's MParent is implicitly one of its may-read entries, and declaring it again
is harmless. A sibling shape skipped every shared ancestor in the read-set test.
**Case.** A describer of database rows cannot enumerate the sidecar files and names the
filesystem instead (`a-store-is-not-one-inode`). A write to a sidecar is a sibling inode,
DISJOINT by the walk, and the walk spares the row fact. The engine cannot tell "my MParent,
redundantly" from "somewhere relative to my MParent".
**Survivor.** The MParent is no may-read entry, and an entry naming a store reaches every MKey
relative to it (2.5-may-read-the-readset).

### refuted-mirror-rule-over-may-read

**Shape.** A write to an MKey is also a write to the may-read entries of its containers.
**Case.** A resolver cache that may-reads `/etc/hosts`. A query that fills the cache would
collide with a fact on that file, and the model could not say that the cache is not held in the
file.
**Survivor.** A write inside a container is a write to it, so the container's own may-write
entailment applies (2.6-may-write-the-writeset).

### refuted-read-side-shared-level-exclusion

**Shape.** A may-read entry that a container declares, at or above the deepest level the
written MKey shares with the read MKey, contributes nothing to the test against that write, by
an engine rule. The write side's exclusion, mirrored.
**Case.** An honest filesystem describer names the block device as a may-read entry. A sibling
inode write then collides every file fact in that filesystem, because nobody has said the device
and a file are different things. The rule removed that collision with no speech behind it.
**Survivor.** The collision is the default. The stdlib keys the device and the filesystem in one
MScheme under a root, and the walk separates them (3.2-compare-one-chokepoint-four-answers). The
write side keeps its exclusion (2.6-may-write-the-writeset).

### refuted-one-hop-dependency-inheritance

**Shape.** A fact's readset is its marked reads and the may-read entries of its own
MFullyQualifiedKey's members, and no more.
**Case.** A loop filesystem whose image lives in another loop filesystem
(`backing-is-not-presenting`). A write to the outer image compares DISJOINT with the inner
fact's inode and with the inner filesystem's image, and the fact is spared. The outer write
rewrote both.
**Survivor.** Each entry adds the entries of its own MFullyQualifiedKey's members until no entry
joins (2.5-may-read-the-readset).

## § 4-routing-and-regions

### refuted-authored-region-predicates

**Shape.** An owner-authored predicate answers whether two regions are disjoint, or a subtree
selector names a region.
**Case.** A symlink retarget. Separation of two objects says nothing about whether a change to
one retargets an MKey for the other. The natural unit of description is the one that a write
disturbs atomically.
**Survivor.** Emitted MTraversals and the region test
(2.9-the-traversal-and-the-region-test).

### refuted-resolution-backed-by-its-target

**Shape.** The object that an identity binding reaches is the binding's backing.
**Case.** The same symlink retarget (`a-path-is-not-a-referent`). The object does not change,
and the name reaches another object.
**Survivor.** A MResolution's backing is its MTraversal (1.7-resolution-and-its-traversal).

### refuted-upward-lookup-as-a-second-scheme

**Shape.** The lookup from a thing to the group that contains it is a second MScheme of the
thing's MSort.
**Case.** MSchemes stay singular, one way of writing a path. Two MSchemes over one class of
strings are meant to conflict safely and never to cohere. Membership is a relation between two
MReferents, never a spelling of one.
**Survivor.** `:places` and its lookup (2.10-places-the-upward-lookup).

### refuted-identity-by-natural-key

**Shape.** A natural key with no resolvable primary key compares by its own MScheme's warrants,
so identity extends to every natural key.
**Case.** A name a store does not hold yet: a file before `touch`, a user before `useradd`. The
name needs identity, and the same reasoning would give every natural key identity. Natural keys
alias: a path through symlinks and hardlinks, a package `provides`.
**Survivor.** Natural keys never carry identity. A describer who wants identity by name mints an
MSort whose primary MScheme is that name in the store that admits it, under warrants the
describer declares (2.2-primary-of-and-identified-in). A secondary MScheme's warrants license
the engine to skip a lookup for equal inputs and nothing more (2.1-yields-into-another-scheme).

## names-never-to-use

- For the store: "table". Also "record", "row", "auxiliary", "weak"; "subsort", "type menu",
  "index types". All are NACKED or never used.
- For the question half of a cell: MClaim (collides with 1.11-site-and-claim-species and the
  spike's `Claim<Tier,_>` tiers, where a fact IS a claim by observation); MExpression
  ("expression" cannot carry two meanings beside sh syntax); MProperty (JavaScript, Swift, and
  Kotlin read it as a stored member); condition (boolean only); attribute (stored-flavoured);
  "observer". The marked line is "a read"; the fact's identity is the MTopic.
- For the stored half of a cell: MField. No novel term may double over a first-class, sorted,
  tracked referent, whatever graph it sits in.
- For the parent: "bearer", MSort-Bearer, "receiver". For a cell: the MCell tag.
- For an MValue: "string".
- For the store's-end closure verb: anything built on "own", "presents", or "home".
