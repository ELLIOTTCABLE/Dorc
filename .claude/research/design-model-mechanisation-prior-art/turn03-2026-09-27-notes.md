# turn 3 — 2026-09-27 12:06:34 CDT

Subagent turn, front-structural-ties-between-instruments. The question: has anyone tied a bounded-model-finding design model (Alloy, Kodkod, Forge, Electrum or equivalent) structurally or provably to a bounded model checker, a proof assistant, or code, so that the same statements are checked by more than one instrument without a hand-maintained seam? Secondary: where the seam cannot be removed, what is the strongest known discipline for checking it, and how do such seams fail.

## Findings

- Finder and prover share literally one statement only where the finder runs inside the prover's own logic, or where both tools read one language. Three instances:
   - Isabelle's Nitpick negates the Isabelle goal and translates the Isabelle definitions into Kodkod's relational logic under an enumerated scope. It runs automatically on every new formula. Because infinite types are approximated, it reports "genuine" versus "potential" counterexamples, and it has an explicitly unsound mode [A-blanchette-nitpick-kodkod-isabelle-2010].
   - Event-B/Rodin: the ProB constraint solver runs on Rodin's own proof obligations as a disprover and as a prover [A-krings-prob-disprover-rodin-2015].
   - TLA+: TLC (explicit-state), Apalache (SMT, bounded and inductive) and TLAPS (proofs) all read one spec with no translation between them [A-konnov-tla-trifecta-tlc-apalache-tlaps-2022].
- The Lean-side counterparts found are weaker:
   - Plausible is random property testing on the goal term itself [A-lean-plausible-readme-2026].
   - Nunchaku (Nitpick's cross-assistant successor) had a Lean frontend only as a plan [A-cruanes-nunchaku-dependent-types-2016].
- Even inside one language, the tools do not share the whole of it. TLC, Apalache and TLAPS each reject a different fragment. The 2022 paper that applies all three to one spec calls itself the first to do so. Its authors used the model checkers on putative invariants in the middle of a proof, and stated library lemmas without proof [A-konnov-tla-trifecta-tlc-apalache-tlaps-2022].
- A bounded check can itself be a theorem when the search is provably exhaustive:
   - ProB tracks infinite enumeration and accepts an exhaustive no-counterexample search as a proof [A-krings-prob-disprover-rodin-2015].
   - Lean's `bv_decide` pipeline: reflect the goal, bitblast with a verified bitblaster, check CaDiCaL's LRAT certificate with a checker proved sound in Lean. The compiled check is admitted as an axiom, which adds the Lean compiler to the trusted base [A-lean-bvdecide-api-docs-2026].
   - A 2026 preprint generalises this to any CNF. It states that the resulting theorem is about exactly the refuted CNF. It concludes that the encoding from the intended statement to the CNF must be written and proved in Lean, or the result is "a true theorem about the wrong formula" [B-szeider-lrat-catcher-lean-theorems-2026].
- Alloy-family models have been translated into provers at least five times, each as a one-off research tool:
   - PVS / Dynamite, 2007 [A-frias-dynamite-alloy-pvs-2007]
   - KeY / Kelloy, 2012 [A-ulbrich-kelloy-alloy-proof-assistant-2012]
   - classical B and Atelier B / Alloy2B, 2018 [A-krings-alloy-to-b-translation-2018]
   - Coq, 2019 [A-souaf-alloy-to-coq-translation-2019]
   - Lean 4 / Lforge, from Forge, 2024 [B-chen-lforge-forge-in-lean-2024]
- The five translations differ in how the prover side is tied back to Alloy:
   - Only Dynamite claims a semantics-preserving interpretation theorem (complete calculus via fork algebras) and calls the Analyzer automatically inside the proof, on every hypothesis introduced by `case` [A-frias-dynamite-alloy-pvs-2007].
   - Kelloy states correctness and relative-completeness theorems [A-ulbrich-kelloy-alloy-proof-assistant-2012].
   - Alloy2B was validated empirically [A-krings-alloy-to-b-translation-2018].
   - The Coq and Lforge translations have no correctness argument. The Coq authors argue that a proof would be near-circular [A-souaf-alloy-to-coq-translation-2019][B-chen-lforge-forge-in-lean-2024].
- In the Alloy-to-prover translations, the semantic mismatches fall in the same three places:
   - Integers. Alloy uses bounded bit-width integers. Kelloy and Lforge deliberately switch to mathematical integers [A-ulbrich-kelloy-alloy-proof-assistant-2012][B-chen-lforge-forge-in-lean-2024]. Alloy2B reports an Alloy 4.2 counterexample, produced by overflow, which ProB correctly shows is not one [A-krings-alloy-to-b-translation-2018].
   - Finiteness. Lforge has to add `Fintype` and `Inhabited` axioms per sig. Without them a property Forge finds UNSAT at every bound (injective `next` plus a root) cannot be proved in Lean [B-chen-lforge-forge-in-lean-2024].
   - Multiplicities. Arrow multiplicities, cardinality, and `one`/`lone` sigs are dropped or left to hand-written axioms [A-souaf-alloy-to-coq-translation-2019][B-chen-lforge-forge-in-lean-2024].
- Effort figures from the proof-side ties, each from one case:
   - Dynamite: a new graduate took 40 days for 285 lemmas [A-frias-dynamite-alloy-pvs-2007].
   - Kelloy: 12 of 22 assertions were proved automatically; the hardest took about one work day; a KeY novice needed two days [A-ulbrich-kelloy-alloy-proof-assistant-2012].
   - Lforge: a mutex spec of about 70 lines needed a proof of about 250 lines [B-chen-lforge-forge-in-lean-2024].
   - TLAPS: the Safra inductive invariant took about 230 lines in one person-day [A-konnov-tla-trifecta-tlc-apalache-tlaps-2022].
- The pluggable-evidence Alloy checker (SCC) plugged in a custom SMT-based EPICS verifier and PLC static analysers through uninterpreted evidence predicates. Its authors call the result sound only up to the bound and "modulo any bugs in the implementation of SCC and the pluggable checkers" [A-pernsteiner-safety-case-pluggable-checkers-2016].
- Model to code, generation versus observation:
   - TestEra uses one Alloy pre/postcondition as both test generator and test oracle for Java. Its model-to-code translations are generated automatically only when the spec sits at the code's representation level. Once the spec abstracts, the translations are hand-written: about 8 man-hours for the INS study [A-khurshid-testera-alloy-java-testing-2004].
   - MongoDB, test generation: generating tests from a TLA+ spec transcribed from about 1000 lines of C++ gave 100% branch coverage [A-davis-extreme-modelling-mongodb-2020].
   - MongoDB, trace-checking: checking the server against a 345-line design-level spec was abandoned after 10 engineer-weeks, with 252 of the 345 lines changed. The authors state that design specs and conformance specs pull in opposite directions, and that no up-front investment makes the per-spec cost cheap [A-davis-extreme-modelling-mongodb-2020].
- TLA+ trace validation is the strongest seam discipline found in production use:
   - Traces carry partial updates to spec variables, and TLC reconstructs the rest.
   - It found discrepancies in every case study. It runs in CCF's CI and is used by etcd.
   - It checks that the trace and the spec share at least one behaviour. It does not check refinement, and a trace that records too little can be accepted wrongly [A-cirstea-tla-trace-validation-2024].
- Cedar, the Lean-model-versus-Rust counter-thesis already in `sources.json`:
   - Differential random testing found 21 bugs.
   - It missed a non-termination bug, and parser bugs outside the generators' scope.
   - One model-versus-code disagreement (the meaning of string size) was resolved by removing the feature from the language [A-disselkoen-cedar-verification-guided-2024].
- Seam failure, empirically:
   - A study of three verified distributed systems found 16 bugs and none in verified protocol code; all sat at interfaces [A-fonseca-verified-distsys-bug-study-2017]:
      - a specification omission let a 7-line patch that disabled deduplication still verify;
      - a Coq client assertion restated its own branch condition, so it could never fail;
      - a build tool reported success when the prover crashed.
   - ProB's inconsistency check found contradictory hypotheses (events that can never fire) in published Event-B models that had been fully proven [A-krings-prob-disprover-rodin-2015].
   - Nitpick found counterexamples to five formulas that the TPS or LEO-II provers had "proved" [A-blanchette-nitpick-kodkod-isabelle-2010].
- kimberlite, a C-grade specimen, runs TLC, TLAPS, Alloy, Ivy, Coq and Kani with no mechanical tie between them. Its hand-maintained matrix [C-kimberlite-fv-traceability-matrix-2026] and its CI [C-kimberlite-formal-verification-ci-2026] show:
   - The matrix credits omitted TLAPS proofs to bounded Alloy, TLC and Ivy checks as "sound mechanical proofs".
   - The matrix records that its own per-row TLAPS tags are wrong, pending reconciliation.
   - The matrix lists Ivy and Kani as PR-blocking. The workflow at the same commit skips both on pull requests and marks them continue-on-error.
   - The workflow's summary job checks only TLC, Alloy and Coq, although its own comment lists TLAPS as required. The TLAPS job still runs, as a separate job.
- Agreement between independent toolchains is itself used as evidence:
   - The ProB authors cite agreement of two or three independently developed provers as high confidence, and describe sending unsat cores to other provers as a second toolchain [A-krings-prob-disprover-rodin-2015].
   - Alloy2B names "a second toolchain to validate the results of the Alloy Analyzer" as a purpose [A-krings-alloy-to-b-translation-2018].
   - The Nunchaku authors call relying on a single counterexample strategy "a mistake" [A-cruanes-nunchaku-dependent-types-2016].
