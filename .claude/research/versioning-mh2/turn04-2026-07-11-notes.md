## Rulings/positions (HUMAN-TYPED this turn)

- vers4-ru-human-versions-the-unanalyzable (the principle, NACK — second — on any
  "don't-need-a-human" framing of version marks): the machine can tell when a human updates
  their TYPE-annotations — that is what a typesystem is; types don't need versions. The
  machine CANNOT tell when a human updates their SEMANTIC ASSUMPTIONS; so semantic
  assumptions DO need versions — human-ack'd, human-updated, as speech-acts. The version
  mark updates in the same commit as the invisible, unanalyzable re-verification work it
  attests (the React-10→11 example: the commit contains (1) the container-rewrite making
  React 11 safe and (2) every consuming callsite flipped to `call@2`). Reading of the
  memcpy incident: typing "memcpy22" MEANS "I read the docs; I rely on
  overlapping-ranges-legal; that reliance is load-bearing in my code." A single, holistic
  stamp-all-current-versions CLI-moment (link-time; install-time) is brownfield-hostile and
  a pinning-footgun: it converts a fundamentally-unanalyzable claim about algorithmic
  semantics and inter-author expectations into a machine-act — the harm-CAUSOR, not the
  harm-fixer. (Offered as defense of the pre-Dorc design, possibly not Dorc-relevant —
  conductor: it is; see vers4-fd-division-of-labor.)
- vers4-ru-two-compilations: compile-intent-to-hashes is a DIFFERENT compilation of author
  intent, and legitimately invisible: a hash has no semantic meaning and no user knowledge
  ("no user-intent bandwidth"). Its role is defensive encoding of the UNDECLARED half of a
  declaration: the user declares "I checked; this held on Python 2.4"; they also meant, but
  had no bandwidth to declare, "…as compiled by the Debian maintainers and shipped to apt
  on 2021-08-03." If that context isn't mechanically encoded on both ends, failures of the
  undeclared half get attributed to the user's declared intent ("doesn't hold on non-Debian
  Python 2.4") — mis-attribution, the cardinal harm-class.
- vers4-ru-oracle-not-book: version-marks are NOT the right route in the book; they ARE
  valuable in the oracle. Human's mapping (asked for confirmation): this end is more the
  selecting-the-right-oracle / handling-oracle-rot end, less the tool-version end.
  Conductor's answer: yes, with one sharpening — vers4-fd-domain-mapping.
- vers4-ru-toctou-defocus: probe-time-vs-apply-time binary re-verification is gently
  DEFOCUSED under the standing mostly-WONTFIXed-TOCTOU posture — "not valueless, just not
  my personal focus at the moment." (Not a nack.)

## Conductor-derived consequences

- vers4-fd-division-of-labor (the corrected split; supersedes the turn03 strawman's tooling
  flow where wrong): three jobs, three owners —
  1. HUMAN authors the semantic marks: the version-window args, the per-arm contract
     claims. Updating a window IS the speech-act "I re-verified against the new contract";
     tooling may PROPOSE (plan-shaped), but the act rides a human commit. The turn03
     strawman's updpkgsums-style in-place rewrite of window args is hereby marked WRONG for
     the window half — it would machine-act a semantic claim (the memcpy harm-causor,
     exactly).
  2. MACHINE stamps the grounding: observed version-string/build/distro/hash arms —
     zero-bandwidth context, generate-by-default, silently updatable. The f2
     bot-UX corpus (updpkgsums, autobump, fake-hash-then-copy) applies to THIS half only.
  3. MACHINE checks and glues: detects presence/consistency/reached-ness of the human's
     marks (the typesystem role — never minting their meaning) and emits the gate into
     probes/applies at point X with rc Y ⇒ Z (user-authors, we-glue; the silent-enforce
     lean covers checking, never authoring).
- vers4-fd-domain-mapping (the "yes?" answered): the machine-stamped half serves the
  selection/rot-ATTRIBUTION end squarely. The human window STRADDLES the two turn01
  domains: it does selection and rot-DETECTION (live outside window ⇒ demote + hint) *and*
  it is a described-tool-contract claim ("this oracle answers for the 2.2-era contract");
  the chronology arms (case-on-captured-version) remain pure domain-2 with no stamps
  needed. ~SUSPECT the deeper unification: an oracle has no independent semantic version of
  its own — its "version" IS its humanly-verified window-set plus its machine stamps;
  oracle-rot = world outside the verified set. (This is why the window-gate strawman felt
  unifying: it is the same vocabulary doing both jobs.)
- vers4-fd-hostile-host-honesty (gentle pushback on one example): the hash-stamp narrows
  the ACCIDENTAL-drift cell (distro rebuilds, non-Debian Python, same-string-different-
  bytes) — but it does NOT defend against the hacked machine: a host that lies to
  `--version` lies to a hash-probe with equal ease; both travel the same forgeable
  reporting channel, and the controller never touches the bytes. The adversarial cell
  stays where it was fenced: the kSTATE/an-host-as-adversary security work. Honest
  division: comparison-window = claim-scoping (no mechanical defense at all); hash-stamp =
  accidental-drift defense; hostile hosts = neither, still open, still fenced.
- vers4-fd-ledger-effects: vers3-ru-window-gate-approved STANDS (the shape is untouched);
  turn03's q2-res-observed-pins rider stands and is now precisely scoped (machine stamps
  only); the `dorc pin bump` flow splits into propose-window (human ack, semantic) vs
  refresh-stamps (machine, automatic); vers2-syn-7's generate-by-default law is rescoped to
