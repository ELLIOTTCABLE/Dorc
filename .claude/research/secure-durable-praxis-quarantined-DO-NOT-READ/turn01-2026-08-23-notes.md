# turn 1 — 2026-08-23 16:52:02 CDT

Front: `front-shopping-posture`. Half A (is there a whole package) and Half B (how to take a
security-critical dependency safely), re-briefed mid-turn to make authored SEAMS a first-class
ranking axis.

## Findings

- The glue thesis has a measured base rate: 83% of cryptographic CVEs are applications misusing
  crypto libraries, 17% are in the libraries themselves. Honest caveat below — the 83% is broader
  than "framing seams".
- No single maintained package covers our whole shopping list (container + per-field encryption +
  key lifecycle + storage primitives) for a cross-platform Rust CLI. That is a firm negative result.
- The negative result does not defeat the whole-package posture, because the seam that actually
  kills is narrow and specific: the join between the encryption layer and the format around it.
  Packages exist that own exactly that join, even though none owns the whole list.
- Closest design fit is the AWS Database Encryption SDK; it *is* shipped in Rust and *does* work
  fully offline under a raw AES keyring. Taking it whole costs 19MB of Dafny-transpiled source plus
  `aws-config`, `aws-sdk-dynamodb`, `aws-sdk-kms`, `tokio`, `chrono`, `cpu-time`, `dashmap` — network
  clients and three clock sources inside a library wanted for a local file.
- Its *design* is the single most transferable artifact found: canonicalised node path as AAD, a
  distinct key per field, verify-before-decrypt, no in-band algorithm negotiation, domain separation
  of every hash use, an authenticated legend of which fields are encrypted, header key-commitment,
  and per-agent MACs so one key holder cannot silently re-author for another.
- SOPS is the decade-shipping existence proof that the rich projection works in production, with a
  near-identical construction: per-value AES-GCM under one file data key, concatenated key path as
  AEAD additional data, plus a tree-wide MAC.
- A third design removes the encryption-versus-format join entirely: SD-JWT keeps the skeleton in
  plaintext and replaces each sensitive value with a digest over a uniquely-salted disclosure held
  separately. Its rule of digesting the *exact serialised bytes* rather than a re-serialisation kills
  the whole canonicalisation-disagreement attack class by construction.
- Recommended synthesis (`turn01:rec-split-commit-and-blob`): a plaintext structural document
  carrying salted commitments, plus one whole-encrypted opaque blob holding the values. One authored
  seam, and it is a digest binding rather than an AEAD binding. Both halves bought off the shelf.
  Eyeball-readability preserved. The plain projection falls out for free by shipping the skeleton alone.
- Composition failures are real, repeatable, and independent of primitive strength: optional or
  in-band-negotiable integrity gets ignored, stripped, or downgraded; signatures get stripped from the
  surrounding container; encrypt-then-sign and sign-then-encrypt are each broken in a different direction.
- KDBX explicitly disclaims its own per-field inner encryption as security-irrelevant, and makes it
  order-dependent across one continuous keystream. A concrete warning against naive per-field encryption.
- AEAD is not a commitment. AES-GCM and ChaCha20-Poly1305 are not key-committing.
- Half B, most decisive datum: the 2026-08-20 `arrayref` compromise was a *popular, legitimate,
  maintained* crate republished through compromised author credentials, live for 86 to 107 minutes.
  Popularity and maintainedness would not have helped. A publication-age quarantine would have
  defeated it completely.
- xz defeated every heuristic at once: payload only in release tarballs and not in git, riding in
  through build machinery, disguised as test fixtures, planted by a long-trusted co-maintainer, and
  found by a performance anomaly rather than any scanner.
- `unsafe_code = "forbid"` on our own crates is measured as "not that meaningful in practice" because
  transitive dependencies still carry unsafe. The constraint is real but buys less than it appears to.
- Transitive review is more tractable than feared: 3434 of the top 10K crates are effect-free, 85% of
  effects sit in 3% of crates, and tool-assisted auditing of `hyper` plus its graph drops to a median
  0.4% of lines.
- 30.8% of effects are safe only depending on calling context. Per-package audit schemes structurally
  cannot see those — the dependency-graph analogue of the seam problem.

## Half A — ranked candidates, by seams we would have to author

Seam count means joins *we* write and own. A crypto seam is a place where metadata, framing, length,
or associated-data handling binds two cryptographic or format components together.

**1. Split artifact: salted commitments in a plaintext skeleton + one whole-encrypted value blob.**
Authored crypto seams: one, and it is a digest binding, fully specified by [A-rfc9901-sd-jwt-selective-disclosure-2026]
including the salt rule and the no-canonicalisation trick. Encryption is bought entire from
[A-c2sp-age-format-spec-2026] (rage in Rust), which owns key wrapping, header MAC, and chunked
authenticated streaming as one reviewed unit. Covers questions 1, 2, 3 and 5 at once. What it costs:
the values are no longer individually addressable inside the file, so a reader wanting one field
decrypts the blob; and the skeleton's metadata leakage becomes explicit and permanent. What it lets us
stop shopping for: per-field AEAD construction, nonce management, AAD design, key wrapping, and the
commitment-versus-digest question — all answered by two specifications neither of which we author.

**2. AWS DB-ESDK taken whole.** Authored crypto seams: zero. Formally verified in Dafny, shipped to
Rust, offline-capable via the raw AES keyring, and its message format was designed by a named
cryptography engineer whose rationale is public [A-arciszewski-db-esdk-innovations-2023]. What taking
it whole costs: our record model must be expressed as DynamoDB attribute maps (a semantic seam, if not
a cryptographic one), and we inherit `tokio`, two AWS service SDKs, and three clock sources against a
hard DST-hermeticity constraint [B-aws-db-esdk-rust-crate-deps-2026]. That constraint is what demotes
it from first place, not any doubt about the cryptography.

**3. SOPS-shaped per-value encryption, via `rops` in Rust.** Authored crypto seams: zero if we adopt
its tree model wholesale. Covers questions 1, 2, 3, 5. What it costs: the model is a text tree
(YAML/JSON/INI), so exact binary preservation needs base64 and bounded streaming decode is not on
offer; and the Rust implementation is thin — the `rops` repository shows 97 stars, one principal
author, and last push 2026-07-13 (GitHub API, checked this turn). For security-critical code the
posture ruling argues *against* this, not for it: it is a small single-maintainer package wearing a
mature design.

**4. COSE, or a hand-assembled RustCrypto stack.** Authored crypto seams: several to many. This is the
option the posture ruling exists to prevent, and the seam literature supports that ruling directly.

**Not viable as take-whole, with reasons:** Tink has the right misuse-resistance philosophy
[B-tink-security-usability-goals-2024] but the Rust port is explicitly disclaimed as not an official
Google product, so its seam-safety is not purchasable from Rust. KDBX is a complete shipped package
[A-keepass-kdbx-format-spec-2026] but is a password database, and its per-field layer is disclaimed.
Cryptomator [B-cryptomator-security-architecture-2026] is a virtual-filesystem vault in Java and
Swift, the wrong product shape and the wrong language. The systemd journal
[A-systemd-journal-file-format-2026] is the right *shape* — append-only, structured, corruption-tolerant,
sealed — but is Linux-only C whose maintainers explicitly tell third parties not to parse it.

## Half B — the dependency-acceptance practice I would recommend

Grounded in what people who do this seriously actually do, and in what the two live incidents show
would and would not have helped.

1. **Quarantine by publication age before anything else.** The single highest-value control found. The
   `arrayref` malicious versions were live 86 to 107 minutes [A-rust-arrayref-supply-chain-attack-2026];
   `cargo`'s `min-push-age` addresses exactly this and currently defaults to zero
   [C-rust-forum-dependency-security-2026]. Costs nothing, no readability impact, defeats the most
   recent real attack outright.
2. **Committed lockfile, no automatic upgrades, and read the diff on every upgrade.** This is Cox's
   watch-your-dependencies discipline [A-cox-software-dependency-problem-2019] and it is the only
   unilateral defence a consumer had when serde began shipping a build-time binary
   [B-serde-precompiled-binary-issue-2023].
3. **Named criteria, with a stricter one for crypto.** `cargo-vet` ships this idea and its own worked
   example of a custom criterion is literally `crypto-reviewed`; Google publishes a `ub-risk-0..4`
   scale that can record a crate as *judged unacceptable* rather than merely unaudited
   [B-cargo-vet-audit-criteria-2026].
4. **Accept `unsafe` in a dependency only under Chromium's conditions** — small, encapsulated,
   documented, and reviewed by someone fluent in the unsafe subset [A-chromium-rule-of-two-2026]. This
   matters because our own `forbid(unsafe_code)` does not extend to the graph
   [A-zoghbi-auditing-rust-crates-2026].
5. **Review by effect, not by line.** Prefer parser and format crates that touch no filesystem,
   network, environment, or clock; that class is a third of the popular ecosystem and can be accepted
   nearly automatically [A-zoghbi-auditing-rust-crates-2026]. Treat build scripts and proc macros as
   the actual attack surface — both live incidents rode in through build-time execution
   [A-freund-xz-backdoor-disclosure-2024], [A-rust-arrayref-supply-chain-attack-2026].
6. **Wrap every accepted dependency behind a thin interface of our own** so it stays replaceable
   [A-cox-software-dependency-problem-2019]. Note the tension flagged below: a wrapper is itself a seam.

### The strongest counter-evidence against the take-the-package posture

Recorded plainly, as evidence, per the round's discipline.

- **Popularity and active maintenance are not evidence of safety, and the two most recent
  compromises prove it.** xz was backdoored by a trusted co-maintainer over years
  [A-freund-xz-backdoor-disclosure-2024]; `arrayref` was a legitimate popular crate republished with
  malware after credential compromise [A-rust-arrayref-supply-chain-attack-2026]. Every "battle-tested"
  heuristic a shopping posture leans on failed in at least one of these.
- **The 83% figure is broader than the glue thesis needs.** [A-lazar-why-crypto-software-fails-2014]
  classifies 223 application-layer bugs, but a large share are forgot-to-encrypt, hard-coded keys, and
  inadequate certificate checks rather than framing or associated-data seams. The direction of the
  human's claim is well supported; the specific claim that *framing* dominates is not established by
  this paper, and I did not find a source that establishes it quantitatively.
- **The minimal-dependency school's best argument is the same argument, pointed the other way.**
  Fewer parts, fewer joins [C-rust-forum-dependency-security-2026]. Where a package is small and
  single-maintainer — as `rops` is — taking it whole buys a mature design wrapped around thin
  maintenance, which is the worst of both.
- **Per-package audit cannot see cross-package seams.** 30.8% of effects are safe only in context, and
  a real CVE arose precisely because responsibility for a path check sat ambiguously between two crates
  [A-zoghbi-auditing-rust-crates-2026]. Buying a package does not buy safety at *its* boundary with us.

## Readability versus security

### Genuinely in tension

- **The plaintext skeleton is the leak.** Field presence, counts, ordering, and lengths are exactly
  what makes the file debuggable by eye and exactly what is disclosed forever. SD-JWT spends decoy
  digests and mandatory shuffling to buy back precisely these
  [A-rfc9901-sd-jwt-selective-disclosure-2026], while the intended design deliberately wants bounded
  counts visible. This cannot be solved, only budgeted and stated.
- **Unknown-field retention versus junk tolerance.** Retaining unknown fields as opaque report material
  is a debuggability feature; Efail shows that a format's tolerance for meaningless content is what lets
  injected garbage be absorbed harmlessly by the parser and ignored by the renderer
  [A-poddebniak-efail-exfiltration-2018]. Mild here, but real, and it needs an explicit answer.
- **Compression.** It makes large records readable and is named as the open composition problem
  (compress-then-encrypt, CRIME) [A-lazar-why-crypto-software-fails-2014], while also making
  malleability attacks harder [A-poddebniak-efail-exfiltration-2018]. Genuinely two-directional.
- **Wrapping a dependency for replaceability** [A-cox-software-dependency-problem-2019] adds a join we
  author, which is what the seam thesis warns against. Both are right; the resolution is that a wrapper
  which passes values through untouched is cheap, and one that reconstructs framing or associated data
  is not.

### NOT in tension — the round's easy keeps

Every item below is good on both axes. None costs readability; several improve it.

- **Verify before decrypt, always** (the Cryptographic Doom Principle)
  [A-arciszewski-db-esdk-innovations-2023]. Free.
- **No in-band algorithm negotiation.** One algorithm per format version; new algorithms mean a new
  version, reviewed before shipping [A-arciszewski-db-esdk-innovations-2023]. This makes the file
  *more* readable, not less — fewer variants to reason about — and it is the direct fix for the
  MDC-stripping and SEIP-to-SE downgrade family [A-poddebniak-efail-exfiltration-2018].
- **Digest the exact serialised bytes; never canonicalise then digest**
  [A-rfc9901-sd-jwt-selective-disclosure-2026]. Less code, fewer bugs, and it deletes an entire attack
  class.
- **Domain-separate every use of a hash function** [A-arciszewski-db-esdk-innovations-2023]. Free.
- **A unique random salt per committed value, 128 bits**
  [A-rfc9901-sd-jwt-selective-disclosure-2026]. This is the settled answer to the low-entropy
  commitment worry and it costs one field.
- **An authenticated legend of which fields are encrypted or omitted**
  [A-arciszewski-db-esdk-innovations-2023]. Improves debuggability *and* stops legend mutation, and it
  is the same object as the intended projection-drops record. A pure win on both axes.
- **Bind the format version under the authentication tag so downgrade cannot pass silently**
  [B-cryptomator-security-architecture-2026]. Free.
- **Check exact expected lengths before attempting decryption**
  [A-c2sp-age-format-spec-2026]. Free, and blunts partitioning oracles
  [A-len-partitioning-oracle-attacks-2021].
- **Reject non-canonical encodings at the parser** [A-c2sp-age-format-spec-2026]. Free.
- **Commit to a deliberately trivial grammar and enforce its limitations**
  [A-chromium-rule-of-two-2026]. Improves readability by definition.
- **Publication-age quarantine, committed lockfile, diff review on upgrade.** Zero readability cost.

## Deliberately not pursued

- The other five fronts' specific questions — wire formats, AEAD constructions, OS keystore APIs,
  filesystem primitives — read only far enough to rank packages. Left for their fronts.
- Whole-database encryption (SQLCipher and relatives): ruled out early, since whole-file encryption is
  excluded by the design and a mutable database is the wrong shape for immutable single-file receipts.
- Backup repositories (restic, borg, Kopia): surveyed, not read in full. They encrypt whole
  content-addressed repositories with no plaintext structural layer, so they cannot answer the
  readability requirement, and their value would be storage-primitive detail belonging to front 5.
- The Invisible Salamanders paper: downloaded and not read. Its result reaches me adequately through
  [A-len-partitioning-oracle-attacks-2021], and its own threat model (attacker chooses both keys) is
  further from ours than the partitioning-oracle setting.
- MongoDB client-side field level encryption: dropped once DB-ESDK proved to be the same idea, done
  later, with published rationale and a Rust target.
- **Forward-secure sealing and the secure-audit-log literature: deliberately deferred, and flagged.**
  This is the correct literature for the "controller account is itself compromised" case, which the
  brief accepts as unwinnable. That acceptance is correct for confidentiality and *not* obviously
  correct for tamper-evidence — forward-secure sealing detects retroactive edits by a principal who
  holds the current key, which is exactly the accepted-loss case. The systemd journal already ships
  this shape [A-systemd-journal-file-format-2026]. Cheap format reservations now (a previous-root
  field) would keep the option open. Recommend a later front, not this one.

## What `front-wire-container` should now ask

The split-artifact option changes the format question materially, so front 2 should settle that first.

1. **Does the container still need per-field encryption at all?** If `turn01:rec-split-commit-and-blob`
   is accepted, the container needs only bounded totally-parseable structure with exact binary fields
   and unknown-field retention — a much easier and much better-served requirement. Ask this before
   evaluating any format.
2. **Can we digest the exact serialised bytes rather than a re-serialisation?** Make this a hard
   selection criterion. A format that forces canonicalisation before hashing hands us a seam we would
   then own forever.
3. **Does the format permit in-band negotiation of algorithm or type that a reader must trust before
   authenticating?** Rule it out by construction: one algorithm per format version.
4. **Does it support an authenticated manifest of what was omitted or hidden**, covered by the same
   authentication as everything else?
5. **How does it retain unknown fields without becoming an arbitrary-content absorber?** Likely answer:
   opaque, counted, length-bounded, and never re-emitted into any rendering context.
6. **Can its Rust implementation be judged effect-free** — no filesystem, network, environment, or clock
   — under the effect model in [A-zoghbi-auditing-rust-crates-2026], and does its `unsafe` (if any) meet
   the small/encapsulated/documented bar of [A-chromium-rule-of-two-2026]?
7. **What is the metadata leakage budget?** Decide explicitly and now what the plaintext skeleton may
   reveal, because it cannot be un-revealed later.
