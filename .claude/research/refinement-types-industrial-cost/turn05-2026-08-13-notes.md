## Findings — the punchline first

- VERDICT: Aeneas-style derived-model verification is credible TODAY for a small carved-out
  arithmetic/algebraic core with owned data; it is NOT credible today for the kernel as described,
  and the single blocking reason is std collections. ~SUSPECT (synthesis).
- DECISIVE: there is **zero** occurrence of `BTreeMap` or `BTreeSet` anywhere in the Aeneas
  repository. Its Lean standard library models only `Vec`, `Slice`, `Array`. A BTreeMap-heavy kernel
  is not consumable without hand-writing a Lean model of the entire used API surface.
  +SURE (github code search, `repo:AeneasVerif/aeneas BTreeMap OR BTreeSet` → total_count 0;
  `path:backends/lean/Aeneas/Std` → Vec.lean, Slice.lean, Array/ArraySlice.lean, Core/FmtWithSlice.lean only)
- HIGHEST-VALUE ACTION NOW, and it is free: put std collections behind a thin owned total facade
  (e.g. a `LatticeMap` newtype over `BTreeMap` exposing ~6 operations). Then the Lean model you would
  eventually owe is YOUR small API, not BTreeMap's. This is also just good engineering. -GUESS on the
  sizing, +SURE on the mechanism. Cedar's precedent (turn01) is that hand-rolling set/map support in
  Lean cost 244 model + 681 proof LOC.
- The separation property the human values is REAL and is Aeneas's actual selling point: proofs never
  touch the Rust. [B-symcrypt-verifying-rust-cryptography-2026] [B-aeneas-glossary-workflow-2026]
- The churn risk is TOOLCHAIN churn, not annotation churn — a different and arguably better-shaped
  risk than the annotation-brittleness lane. But it is live: Charon pins `nightly-2026-06-01`, Aeneas
  pins Charon by commit hash, and "Update charon" merges land roughly weekly.

## Findings — Aeneas scope: what discipline the input Rust must follow

- Safe Rust only. Unsafe and concurrency are excluded outright, pending in-progress separation-logic
  work. [B-aeneas-readme-targeted-subset-2026]
- Current named limitations: `return` inside *nested* loops and `break`/`continue` to *outer* loops;
  and instantiating a generic type (including `Self` via a trait impl) with a mutable reference.
  Both flagged as engineering, not fundamental. [B-aeneas-readme-targeted-subset-2026]
- Traits ARE supported. Generics are supported except the mutable-reference instantiation case —
  but in practice an independent team found generic functions with trait bounds "cannot be extracted
  directly: Aeneas needs concrete monomorphised types", and worked around it by hand-writing a
  monomorphic model. [B-rust-lean-pipeline-ai-provers-2026]
- DOCUMENTATION CONFLICT, flagged: the project's own user manual (`docs/user/src/README.md`, last
  modified 2024-07) is stale and stricter — it claims no nested loops at all, no function
  pointers/closures, no nested borrows in signatures, and no interior mutability. The 2026-07 README
  is narrower. Treat the README as current; treat closures / nested-borrows / interior-mutability as
  probably still restricted but UNVERIFIED. ~SUSPECT — worth a direct question on their Zulip.
- Interior mutability is excluded per the ICFP'22 framing ("as long as they do not rely on interior
  mutability or unsafe code"). ~SUSPECT (secondary; not read from the paper this turn).
- The frontend is self-described alpha with two known unsoundnesses and planned breaking changes;
  bodies of std and foreign-crate functions are absent from the translated output.

## Findings — the workflow, and LLM fit

- Generated vs hand-written is cleanly separated: `Types.lean` and `Funs.lean` are generated and
  overwritten; `FunsExternal.lean` (hand-written std/external models), `Spec.lean`, and `Properties/`
  are yours. [B-aeneas-glossary-workflow-2026] [B-aeneas-readme-targeted-subset-2026]
- What you actually manipulate: every function lands in a `Result` monad (ok/fail/div, where fail
  covers panic/overflow/out-of-bounds); `&mut T` becomes a backward continuation with type
  `Result (T × (T → Result OriginalType))`; loops become a `loop` fixed-point combinator over
  `ControlFlow`. [B-aeneas-glossary-workflow-2026]
- Tactic surface is substantial and Aeneas-specific: `step`/`step*`/`step*?`, `scalar_tac`,
  `simp_scalar`, `simp_lists`, `bv_tac`, `bvify`, `natify`, `zmodify`, `agrind`.
  [B-aeneas-glossary-workflow-2026]
- LLM fit is unusually good and the ecosystem is already built for it: the Aeneas repo ships a
  `CLAUDE.md` stating "All detailed instructions for AI agents live in `documentation/skills/`", and
  2026 commits carry `Co-authored-by: Claude Opus 4.8` and note Gemini used for debugging.
  +SURE (ghSearchCode + commit log)
- SymCrypt uses agents for two jobs — drafting Lean specs from standards, and writing/maintaining
  proofs including repairing them after refactors — with the Lean kernel as the trust boundary.
  Crucially: "Agents do not need to annotate or modify the production Rust implementation."
  [B-symcrypt-verifying-rust-cryptography-2026]
- The only independent empirical split of what AI closes vs what stays manual: AI reliably closed
  control-flow/structural lemmas, linear arithmetic, and simp-boilerplate around extracted code.
  Manual: domain-specific algebraic identities, **loop invariants on Aeneas-extracted recursive
  functions**, and choosing axioms for external interfaces. Loop invariants are exactly what a
  fixpoint engine is made of. [B-rust-lean-pipeline-ai-provers-2026]

## Findings — the rest of the no-added-code lane

- **Kani autoharness** is the strongest genuinely-zero-added-code option on ordinary Rust: it scans
  the crate, generates harnesses internally, and "Kani does not make any changes to your source
  code." Two eligibility gates decide everything: every argument must implement or be able to derive
  `Arbitrary` (or be a reference to such), and **generic functions get no harness at all**.
  Experimental, `-Z autoharness`, and it implies `-Z function-contracts` and `-Z loop-contracts`.
  [B-kani-autoharness-reference-2026]
- With zero hand-written anything, autoharness concludes: absence of Kani's supported UB classes
  (out-of-bounds, null/dangling deref, uninitialised memory, arithmetic overflow in unsafe contexts)
  over all bit-valid inputs, bounded by loop unwinding unless loop contracts are present.
  [A-verifying-rust-standard-library-2026]
- TENSION WORTH NAMING: autoharness derives `Arbitrary` at MIR level for structs/enums, but
  "variants with non-public fields or unsupported types cause the derivation to fail". A
  smart-constructor newtype is *by definition* a private field. So the codebase's existing
  newtype discipline partially fights this lane. Fix is cheap: hand-written `Arbitrary` impls behind
  `#[cfg(kani)]`. ~SUSPECT on how often it bites. [A-verifying-rust-standard-library-2026]
- **Prusti** genuinely is in this lane: by default, with zero annotations, it verifies absence of
  panics and of integer overflows. Maintenance status remains UNRESOLVED across turns — not
  established this turn either. Treat as a stranding risk until checked.
- **MIRAI** is in the lane by design — the panic-linting use case "generally requires no
  annotations" — but is effectively dormant: last commit to endorlabs/MIRAI is 2025-03-04, about
  seventeen months stale, in a repo whose issue numbers restarted in the 30s after transfer from
  facebookexperimental. +SURE (commit API). Do not build on it.
- **Hax** (Cryspen) targets Lean/F*/Rocq but is annotation-driven (`#[hax::contract]`), so it is NOT
  in the no-added-code lane; its Lean backend is "under active development" and independent analysis
  has found semantic gaps versus Rust semantics for certain patterns.
  [B-rust-lean-pipeline-ai-provers-2026]

## Findings — the discipline intersection (what the human asked to have named)

Where these tools AGREE. Each item is demanded by two or more of Aeneas / Kani-autoharness /
Prusti-no-spec, and each is defensible as ordinary good engineering:

1. **No `unsafe`.** Aeneas excludes it outright; the others give weaker guarantees inside it.
2. **No interior mutability** (`Cell`/`RefCell`) and **no shared-mutable aliasing**. Aeneas's whole
   advantage comes from Rust's aliasing-xor-mutability; interior mutability destroys it.
3. **No concurrency in the kernel.**
4. **Monomorphic at the verification boundary.** STRONGEST AGREEMENT: Kani autoharness skips generic
   functions entirely; Aeneas needs concrete monomorphised types in practice. Generics internally are
   fine — concrete types on the chokepoint functions are what matters.
5. **Simple, flat loops.** No `return` from nested loops, no labelled `break`/`continue` to outer
   loops (Aeneas hard limit); bounded or contract-annotated loops (Kani). Recursion over
   flat structures is friendlier than clever loop nests to both.
6. **Own your data structures at the boundary.** Charon has no std bodies; Aeneas has no map/set
   models. A thin owned facade over `BTreeMap` converts an unbounded modelling obligation into a
   small one.
7. **Total, panic-free chokepoint functions with constructible argument types.** This is the Kani
   eligibility rule and it is also what makes an Aeneas `Result`-monad proof short.
8. **Values and `&mut` over shared borrows.** `&mut T` has a clean, mechanical translation (backward
   continuation); nested borrows in signatures do not.

+SURE that 1-6 are each individually sourced above; ~SUSPECT that this is the complete intersection —
I did not test the disciplines against Prusti's or ESBMC's demands in detail.
