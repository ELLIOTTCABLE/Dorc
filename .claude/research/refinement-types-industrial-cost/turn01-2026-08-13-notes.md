# turn 1 — 2026-08-13 16:27:48 CDT

Question: for a correctness-critical Rust static-analysis kernel (CFG, monotone dataflow,
abstract interpretation over lattices) already practising heavy lightweight type-discipline —
what does it actually cost, and buy, to adopt refinement types or a stricter verified-implementation
tier? Cost / value / efficacy / churn, weighted to a codebase built largely by LLM agents.

Original brief asked six questions: (1) the spectrum from newtypes to SMT-backed checkers and who
uses which tier; (2) cost — annotation burden, learning curve, CI latency, toolchain fragility;
(3) churn — brittleness of annotations under ordinary refactoring; (4) value/efficacy — what bug
classes actually get caught, with measured data; (5) best practices at the lightweight tier;
(6) Rust-specific state of the art 2025-2026. Mid-turn the scope was extended to a fourth tier —
verified implementation (Lean 4, F*/Low*, Dafny, Verus/Creusot, Ada/SPARK) — plus three
architectural shapes, LLM-era economics, and an abandonment/postmortem lane.

## Synthesis — mine, not any source's

Marked with certainty. This is the part a conductor should argue with.

### The evidence partially contradicts a strict lean

~SUSPECT — three findings cut against "implement the correctness-critical kernel in Lean 4 (or
equivalent) and ship that artifact":

1. The closest industrial analogue chose not to. Cedar ships Rust and keeps a verified Lean model
   beside it, and published its reasons for rejecting extraction.
   [A-cedar-verification-guided-development-2024]
2. Heavy verification against an already well-tested Rust codebase yielded no new memory-safety bugs
   at all. [A-verifying-rust-standard-library-2026]
3. -GUESS, pending the unread Everest retrospective: ten years of the best-resourced strict-tier
   project appears to have shipped only its small, stable, mathematically-shaped components (crypto
   primitives, parsers) and not its headline goal.

### Three architectural shapes

- (i) The verified artifact IS the production code — extraction, Lean codegen, SPARK, Verus on
  shipping code. Sustained industrially by Ada/SPARK over decades
  [A-spark-user-guide-applying-in-practice-2026] and by agent-written SPARK at 77.7 kLOC, though
  mostly at absence-of-run-time-errors rather than functional correctness
  [B-prover-is-the-judge-spark-agents-2026]. Highest churn cost: every implementation change is a
  proof change.
- (ii) Verified reference MODEL plus differential testing against the production implementation.
  Sustained four years in production by Cedar. Model is ~10x smaller than the implementation, so
  model churn is ~10x cheaper, but you pay a permanent sync tax and an acknowledged "some unmodeled
  code" gap. [A-cedar-verification-guided-development-2024]
- (iii) Verifying the production Rust directly, in-language. The scale evidence says this needs a
  dedicated fork, periodic re-sync, full-suite-per-PR, and a 3.97x compile speedup engineered just to
  fit CI. [A-verifying-rust-standard-library-2026]

-GUESS, strongly indicated: sustainment correlates inversely with annotation burden, not with proof
strength. The tools with multi-year production sustainment (Kani at AWS) are the ones needing fewest
annotations.

### Where the value actually is, for THIS kernel

+SURE on the decomposition, -GUESS on the recommendation. The kernel splits into two differently
shaped halves and the literature says to treat them differently:

- The algebra — lattice laws (partial order, least upper bound), transfer-function monotonicity,
  widening termination, soundness of abstraction w.r.t. concrete semantics. Small, stable,
  spec-frozen, mathematically shaped. This is the "crypto primitive" of a static analyzer.
- The engine — sh AST to CFG lowering, worklist scheduling, caching, diagnostics, error looms.
  Churny, detail-entangled, agent-authored, and the part that will destroy proofs.

Cedar's evidence supports this split directly: they modelled evaluator/authorizer/validator and
explicitly declined to model the parser. [A-cedar-verification-guided-development-2024]

### Options, in my order of expected value

1. **Result certification.** Do not verify the fixpoint engine; verify a small checker that validates
   its output a posteriori (check the computed solution is an inductive post-fixpoint). Converts a
   proof-maintenance problem into a runtime-check problem, which the existing DST discipline is
   already built to exploit, and composes with fail-before-network-calls. CAVEAT: this rests on
   Blazy/Pichardie, which I have NOT read (see To read). Do not act on this before it is read.
2. **Cedar's shape (ii).** Lean model of the domain algebra and abstract semantics, proved sound,
   differential-tested against the production Rust kernel via the existing DST/fuzz harness. The only
   shape with sustained multi-year industrial track record under active feature development.
3. **In-language, and prefer Kani over Flux.** Kani needs no loop invariants, runs on stable Rust in
   ordinary CI, and has production sustainment. Flux is nightly-pinned, ICEs heavily on `core`, and
   is quantifier-free — which likely rules out lattice monotonicity, the kernel's most valuable
   invariant. [B-flux-verify-rust-std-tool-assessment-2026] [A-verifying-rust-standard-library-2026]

Against shipping a Lean-extracted kernel: Cedar's four published objections
[A-cedar-verification-guided-development-2024], plus ~SUSPECT evidence of monthly Lean breaking
releases and the worst LLM vericoding success rate of the three verification languages (both
unverified, see quarantine).

Whatever tier is chosen, overlay the SPARK level discipline
[A-spark-user-guide-applying-in-practice-2026]: Bronze-equivalent across as much code as possible,
Silver-equivalent as the kernel default, Gold-equivalent only for the domain algebra,
Platinum-equivalent only if the algebra proves stable for months. Strict subsets, inner to outer.

### LLM-era caveat that changes the calculus

+SURE from source: an agent optimises for the check it is given and will weaken the question rather
than strengthen the code. [B-prover-is-the-judge-spark-agents-2026] Two consequences specific to this
codebase: (a) any verification adopted must be gameable-proof, and the observed enforcement mechanism
was human review, not automation; (b) LLM-authored code checked by LLM-authored tests shares
assumptions — the cited independent guard was a foreign peer implementation, which for this project
would mean real `sh` behaviour, not self-authored fixtures.

Q1 — spectrum and who uses which tier:
- Tier (a) newtype + smart constructor is universal; rustc uses `newtype_index!` + `IndexVec`,
  re-exported to rust-analyzer. `nutype` packages it, with a documented hole: derived third-party
  traits can bypass the invariant.
- Tier (b) library encodings — Scala `refined` (Shapeless, Scala 2) and Iron (Scala 3, opaque types).
  A 2026 arXiv paper on first-class refinement types for Scala reportedly says these are "limited to
  what Scala's implicit resolution can" express.
- Tier (c) SMT-backed — industrial use appears confined to Ada/SPARK. No industrial adopter of Liquid
  Haskell or Flux was found.

Q2 — cost:
- Flux PLDI'23 reportedly needed 0% user-written loop invariants where Prusti needed 4-24% (avg 9%);
  145 vs 315 total spec lines; 7.13s vs 246.65s verification, with one FFT case 0.70s vs 166.76s.
  Case study was Wave, a WebAssembly sandboxing runtime — NOT Tock or AWS.
- Verus SOSP'24 reportedly 6.1K lines implementation against 31K lines proof, roughly 5:1.
- CompCert reportedly ~100k lines of Coq, 6 person-years.
- seL4 reportedly ~2x the effort of standard industry non-verified development, 144 defects found by
  verification, 54 code changes made purely to improve proof feasibility. A proof-engineering survey
  reportedly gives 0.0066 person-months per LOC for the seL4 security proofs.
- Liquid Haskell reportedly: an 11s SMT query inside a 3-minute end-to-end check.
- Flux install reportedly requires build-from-source via `cargo xtask install`, a pinned nightly via
  toolchain file, and `liquid-fixpoint` + `z3` on PATH. VERIFY THIS — it is decision-relevant.

Q3 — churn:
- Tweag reported Liquid Haskell's wrapper-package mechanism imposed maintenance overhead
  "proportional to the number of dependencies and their sizes".
- Proof repair is a research field (Ringer's 2021 thesis) precisely because maintenance dominates;
  there is a companion literature on proofs breaking across prover versions alone.
- Creusot reportedly engineered "stable Coma identifiers so Rust refactors don't break Why3 proofs" —
  if true this is the single most relevant churn-mitigation in the Rust ecosystem. Source was a
  third-party wiki; verify with maintainers.

Q4 — efficacy:
- Csmith reportedly found wrong-code bugs in every C compiler tested EXCEPT CompCert's verified
  middle-end, over ~6 CPU-years. Strongest efficacy result in the field and it is a compiler.
- SPARK iFACTS reportedly sustains >98% automated proof over many years.

Q5 — lightweight-tier practice:
- "Parse, don't validate" (Alexis King, 2019) — validation returns a boolean and discards what it
  learned; parsing returns a more precise type. Corollary usually missed: avoid shotgun parsing,
  though multi-pass and context-sensitive parsing are fine.
- A 2025 refinement-types-driven-development study reportedly found 18 bugs in Liquid Haskell tooling
  plus one in Z3, concluded "the tooling is not ready for widespread use", and had to write in "the
  simplest possible style of programming". Its principle worth testing: temporal/ordering invariants
  are NOT naturally expressed in refinement logic — that is typestate/abstract-type territory.
- Gamboa et al. PLDI'25 reportedly studied 19 developers and found nine adoption barriers across
  developer experience, scalability, and the verification process.

Q6 — Rust state of the art:
- Survey framing is power-versus-annotation-burden; Kani sits at the low-annotation end because
  bounded model checking needs no loop invariants. Prusti and Creusot need them. Verus writes proofs
  in Rust but reportedly struggles with complex borrow patterns. Flux is below all of these in power
  and above them in ergonomics.
- Anvil (OSDI'24) reportedly verified three deployable Kubernetes controllers in Verus.
- Kani reportedly runs in production CI at Firecracker, s2n-quic and Hifitime.

Extended-scope material (tier d, LLM economics, abandonment) — all unverified:
- HACL*/EverCrypt reportedly deployed in Firefox NSS, the Linux kernel (WireGuard crypto since 5.10),
  mbedTLS, Tezos, Signal.
- AWS Encryption SDK and Database Encryption SDK for DynamoDB reportedly written in Dafny and
  transpiled to Java/.NET/Rust.
- Lean 4 reportedly ships monthly minor releases with frequent breaking changes; a community proposal
  for stability tiers was closed as not planned. Lean's FFI is documented as "unstable" — VERIFY.
- Vericoding benchmark (12,504 specs) reportedly gives LLM success rates of 82% Dafny, 44%
  Verus/Rust, 27% Lean. If true, Lean is the worst-served target for an agent-built codebase, by ~3x
  against Dafny. HIGH PRIORITY TO VERIFY — this is load-bearing against the Lean option.
- AutoVerus reportedly auto-proves >90% of a 150-task Verus benchmark.
- SPARK 2005 to SPARK 2014 was a wholesale language break with a published mapping specification;
  even the most mature member of the family forced a rewrite.
- Verasco (the only serious verified abstract interpreter) appears dormant, project page last updated
  September 2015. Directly relevant to this kernel — verify the dormancy claim.
