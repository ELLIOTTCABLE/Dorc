## Findings

- READ IN FULL: Blazy/Laporte/Maroneze/Pichardie SAS'13. The result-certification architecture is
  real, precisely specified, and better-shaped for Dorc than even the turn-1 synthesis guessed:
  the fallback on certificate failure is TOP, not abort. `solve_pfp = if check_fxp ... then fxp
  else top`. In Dorc terms a failed certificate degrades to the stage-0 floor (nothing elides,
  everything runs) — a world-state the product already narrates first-class. The "runtime net"
  therefore lands in tier-2/floor territory, NOT the expensive tier-3 cell.
  [A-blazy-fixpoint-checker-2013]
- The checker validates a POST-fixpoint, per-node, purely locally: apply transfer once, test
  abstract inclusion against the stored solution. The untrusted engine keeps ALL the churny
  heuristics (Bourdoncle strategies, widening/narrowing, loop unrolling, delayed widening,
  decreasing iterations) — they explicitly judged direct verification of Bourdoncle "highly
  challenging" and "too rigid" because widening NEEDS heuristic adjustment in practice.
  [A-blazy-fixpoint-checker-2013]
- The per-domain proof surface is MINIMALIST: three obligations only (gamma_monotone, top_sound,
  join_sound). No widening laws at all (validated a posteriori); no completeness; no
  best-approximation; explicitly "any weak-join will be suitable". Transfer-function MONOTONICITY
  IS NOT REQUIRED for soundness in this architecture — only per-operator gamma-containment.
  Directly defuses the turn-1 worry that lattice monotonicity (a forall-property) must be the
  kernel's central verified invariant: in the certifier architecture it is a precision/termination
  concern, not a soundness obligation. [A-blazy-fixpoint-checker-2013]
- Lightweight-tier lesson from a Coq shop: they DECLINED a dependent invariant (min <= max on
  intervals) as "rather heavyweight in practice" — the invariant rides the concretization
  hypothesis instead. Even proof-assistant users ration refinement-by-construction.
  [A-blazy-fixpoint-checker-2013]
- Cost datapoint: ~7,500 lines Coq (4,000 defs + 3,500 proof) + 200 OCaml, atop CompCert's 100k,
  for a value analysis over a C-expressiveness CFG language; 34s to analyze a ~3,000-line C
  program (10k CFG instrs). Precision within ~11% of industrial Frama-C on their benchmark
  (355 vs 398 bounded variables). [A-blazy-fixpoint-checker-2013]
