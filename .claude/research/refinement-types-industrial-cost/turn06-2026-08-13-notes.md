## The laws analysis, condensed (explainer #1 was delivered in-chat; the durable core)

The kernel splits: ALGEBRA (lattice.rs 480 + solve.rs 301 + claim/coord chokepoints; ~1.2k
lines; spec-frozen; every future implementation needs the same laws — the "crypto primitive"
in Everest's sizing sense) vs ENGINE (effect/value/cfg/funcenv ~11k lines; churny;
correctness needs largely met by structure + DST). The laws split into two DUTIES under
narrative-30: (a) termination/no-answer duty (idempotence, monotonicity, finite height,
semantic Eq — violations oscillate/climb/never-settle; the empirical hang was finite-height
violation: fresh key minted per visit); (b) OUTPUT-WELL-DEFINEDNESS duty (commutativity,
associativity, ⊥-identity, absorption — violations make the result depend on traversal
order, which under narrative-30 is an incorrect OUTPUT on the aid plane, not just an
engineering wart; absorption additionally makes ⊑ well-defined, which elision-safety and the
certifier's per-edge check are phrased in). The certifier escape: "will the engine find an
answer" needs the function-properties (unprovable-in-types, expensive); "is THIS answer safe"
is a finite pile of concrete per-edge ⊑ comparisons over produced values — no quantifiers.
A post-fixpoint is a self-consistent over-approximation; how it was found is irrelevant to
its safety. That is Blazy/Pichardie [A-blazy-fixpoint-checker-2013], with fallback-to-⊤ =
Dorc's stage-0 floor.
