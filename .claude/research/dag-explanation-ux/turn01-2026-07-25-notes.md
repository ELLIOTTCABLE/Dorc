## Findings (lifted)

- **f1. The premise "users don't read it" is FALSE, and that is worse news, not better.** Eye-tracking
  (n=56) shows 13-25% of fixations land on error-message areas, and message-reading is *as
  cognitively hard as reading source code* (419ms vs 394ms mean fixation; silent English 275ms).
  Attention is being spent; the question is whether it buys anything. [A-barik-developers-read-error-messages-2017] +SURE
- **f2. More reading of the message predicts a WORSE outcome.** Revisits to the error-message area
  (the standard proxy for reading difficulty) significantly predict *incorrect* fixes
  (G2=60.9, p<.0001). A render that makes users re-read is a render that is failing.
  [A-barik-developers-read-error-messages-2017] +SURE
- **f3. Messages ANCHOR the solution space, including onto the wrong one.** 53 of 55 participants
  converged on the same *incorrect* fix, because the message's wording steered them there;
  incorrect solutions clustered into a tiny set (nbins 0-3 per task). A chain that names one link
  prominently will be treated as *the* cause. [A-barik-developers-read-error-messages-2017] +SURE
- **f4. Self-report and behaviour diverge sharply on enriched messages.** 78% of students said the
  enhanced messages helped; measured same-error-repeat went 13.71% -> **13.99%**, and mean time
  between submissions rose ~150s -> ~250s. 8 semesters, 36,050 submissions.
  [A-pettit-enhanced-messages-inconclusive-2017] +SURE - this is the sharpest single don't-do:
  *never validate a richer render by asking users if it helped.*
- **f5. Verbose-but-correct loses to short-but-structured, on BOTH objective and subjective measures.**
  n=106 randomized within-subjects: GPT-4 explanations (always containing the correct fix) beat stock
  GCC in only 1 of 6 tasks; expert *handwritten* messages in the rustc shape (`Error:` + source
  excerpt + `Help:`/`Note:`) beat both in 4-5 of 6. Length perception: handwritten "just right"
  88.1%; LLM messages split across all five length buckets.
  [A-santos-llm-messages-not-silver-bullet-2024] +SURE
- **f6. Detail level is not the lever; PLACEMENT and STRUCTURE are.** Direct manipulation of message
  density (short / visual-inline / long): long form gave *no* correctness gain, while visual-inline
  was significantly FASTER (166.9s vs 247.2s short, 223.0s long) - and simultaneously WORST for
  beginners. [B-nienaltowski-what-can-help-novices-2008] ~SUSPECT (small n, one item per cell)
- **f7. One density cannot serve two users - the reversal is measured and large.** Integrated
  text-in-diagram helped novices (d=+1.67, +1.89) and *actively harmed* experts (d=-0.44, -0.88) on
  a fault-finding task; the pattern replicates across ~25 studies. Mechanism: for a reader who
  already holds the schema, the external explanation is redundant and its reconciliation cost is
  pure extraneous load. [A-kalyuga-expertise-reversal-effect-2007] ~SUSPECT for transfer (lab
  instruction, not field diagnosis)
- **f8. The target population explicitly asks for Dorc's chain - and explicitly says it won't go get it.**
  19/20 practitioners want to know *why* a thing was flagged; one spontaneously designs the render
  ("a slice ... what the problem is and what else could be affected", via call hierarchies). The same
  interviews: flow disruption and pane-switching are adoption killers, "a guarantee that unmotivated
  people will not do it." [A-johnson-why-not-static-analysis-tools-2013] +SURE - the direct threat
  to `27V:rul-chain-is-pull-only`.
- **f9. Users navigate causality by lexical SCENT, not by following derivations.** Professionals'
  verbalizations concerned scent-following far more than hypotheses; a scent model out-predicts a
  hypothesis model for where they go next. They also *enrich* (grep, bookmarks, tabs) to cut travel
  cost. [B-lawrance-information-foraging-debugging-2013] ~SUSPECT
- **f10. Barik's own recommended alternative is Dorc's shape.** The paper names LLVM `scan-build` -
  error as "a sequence of steps that the developer can follow alongside in the context of the code" -
  as the promising contrast to conventional decoupled messages.
  [A-barik-developers-read-error-messages-2017] +SURE
- **f14. A carefully-designed controlled vocabulary does NOT get acquired, and a glossary does not
  rescue it.** After 2+ months of daily exposure to DrRacket's deliberately-chosen terms, only 4 of
  15 words were correctly identified by >50% of students (n=163, three universities). Using a word
  in lectures bought +13.8% (CI 2.93–24.7%). A supplied vocabulary guide showed no reliable effect.
  Students also read the code *highlight* as "edit here" when it carries five distinct meanings, and
  routinely mapped it to the `expected` half of an `expected … but found …` message.
  [A-marceau-mind-your-language-vocabulary-2011] +SURE — this is the direct threat to the tier-word
  scheme (measured/vouched/ran/claimed/derived/consented) *and* to `dorc explain` as its remedy.
- **f12. WHERE/WHEN you surface it dominates HOW WELL you explain it — by two orders of magnitude.**
  Identical analysis, identical false-positive rate (<20%): batch deployment saw a ~0% fix rate;
  moving the same reports to code-review time took it to >70%. Named mechanism: mental context
  switch, plus relevance-to-what-you-are-doing-now.
  [A-distefano-scaling-static-analyses-facebook-2019] +SURE — the single largest measured effect
  in this round, and it is not about the render at all.
- **f13. A production tool holding a 61-step derivation renders three lines.** Infer's OpenSSL
  null-deref example has a 61-step interprocedural trace crossing five procedures; the shipped
  message is `file:line: NULL_DEREFERENCE pointer 'revtm' last assigned on line 2778 could be null
  and is dereferenced at line 2780` plus three source lines. Endpoints + the one pivotal fact.
  Separately, Zoncolan uses *trace length* as a suppression/promotion filter — long chains are
  demoted, not elaborated. [A-distefano-scaling-static-analyses-facebook-2019] +SURE
- **f11. Mixed natural-language + code in one line carries a measurable code-switching cost.** Barik's
  proposed explanation for why error messages read harder than source: they are "both natural
  language ... and code, but are not entirely either," forcing a modality switch (citing the bilingual
  code-switching literature). Bears directly on tier-word + inlined-excerpt lines.
  [A-barik-developers-read-error-messages-2017] -GUESS (author's hypothesis, not tested)
