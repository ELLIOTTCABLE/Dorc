# turn 1 — 2026-07-20 15:21:34 CDT

Errand: prior-art for Dorc `dorc explain <slug>` — is INLINE TRANSCLUSION of shared prose into
composed teaching pages, edited THROUGH the composed view, an unoccupied middle because untried, or
because it fails? Poles: (a) duplicate-per-page (rustc), (b) link-out-to-shared (Elm hints, shellcheck
wiki). Gather+grade only; design adjudication is elsewhere.

## Findings (lifted, most-attended first)

- MONEY-ANSWER (provisional, ~SUSPECT): the middle is **mostly untried at THIS exact spec, but the
  general shape has been tried repeatedly and its ONE consistent failure mode is authoring-out-of-context
  — which is precisely the failure Dorc's compose-view authoring is built to neutralise.** The domains
  split: (i) reuse-of-shared-source is the recognised CURE for duplicate-drift (legal assembly, techcomm
  maintenance, Wikipedia {{Sync}} pain); (ii) but mid-size shared-PROSE-paragraph transclusion is exactly
  the granularity that ROTTED in techcomm, always via edit-out-of-context. Nobody has combined
  single-source dedup WITH in-composed-view authoring — that specific cell is genuinely under-occupied.
- fin-survived-granularities-are-extremes: reuse that survives sits at the extremes — whole-file/whole-topic
  OR context-free atoms (tested code snippets, warning/admonition blocks, single substitution variables).
  Mid-size shared prose paragraphs are the rotted middle. [B-tomjohnson-single-sourcing-over-2025],
  [C-stilo-conref-limitations-2023], [B-mdbook-includes-2024], [B-asciidoctor-tagged-regions-2024].
- fin-root-cause-is-out-of-context: every fragment-reuse failure traces to the SAME root — the fragment
  is edited without its assembled surroundings, so coherence/links/headings drift. [A-baker-frankenbooks-must-die-2012],
  [B-baker-chunking-translation-2011], [B-tm-friend-or-foe-deck-2018].
- fin-duplicate-is-the-worse-drift-risk: the strongest legal-drafting evidence says DUPLICATION, not
  sharing, is the primary driver of incoherence/drift; single-source-update is the recognised cure —
  DISCONFIRMS the worry that inline reuse is the risky choice. [B-henley-draft-like-a-pro-2018],
  [B-adams-dysfunction-causes-cure-2013], [B-avvoka-maintenance-at-scale-2026].
- fin-semantic-seam-is-the-deep-risk: the real seam is SEMANTIC not visual — a reused fragment silently
  no longer fits its host (meaning-drift, "encrustation"); error-correction feedback fails exactly where a
  fragment has lost shared meaning. [A-blackhole-boilerplate-choi-gulati-scott-2017].
- fin-wikipedia-default-is-summarize-and-link-accepting-duplication: Wikipedia's dominant norm for shared
  concepts across articles is summarize-in-place + {{Main}} link, EXPLICITLY accepting duplication; prose
  transclusion ({{Excerpt}}/LST) exists but is a cautious, marginal exception. [B-wikipedia-summary-style-2026],
  [B-wikipedia-lst-help-2026].
- fin-parameterization-arc-is-the-cautionary-tale: Wikipedia's params→ParserFunctions→Lua/Scribunto arc
  shows parameterized prose escalating until it left the authorable surface entirely (module namespace,
  a priesthood); pluralization/agreement is exactly what got pushed into Lua. Strongly corroborates Dorc's
  grammatical-composition anti-goal. [B-wikipedia-lua-2026]; and the legal analogue: keep the fragment
  DUMB, push logic out. [B-automationlogs-common-mistakes-2026].
- fin-google-inline-your-own-link-others: canonical style-guide ruling — inline your OWN context (define a
  term, briefly explain a concept), link OUT only for someone-else's domain/standard; "each link is a chance
  for the reader to leave the page and lose their place." Dorc's shared concepts are its OWN content → inline
  side. [A-google-style-cross-references-2025].
- fin-ARID-resolves-the-tension: "Accept some Repetition In Documentation" (strict DRY fails for prose) YET
  "eliminate content overlap between SOURCES" — resolves to single-source / repeated-in-output, which is
  exactly Dorc's decomposed-template / inlined-render model. [A-writethedocs-principles-2024].
- fin-both-views-is-a-real-pattern: memoQ's co-founder argues the right architecture keeps segmented
  storage/lookup AND an unsegmented whole-content preview — decomposed store + composed view together;
  proof the "author in composed view over decomposed units" middle is buildable and defended by practitioners.
  [B-memoq-segmentation-2025].
- fin-AI-authored-explain-docs-hit-the-authoritative-wall: Clang maintainers rejected AI-generated
  diagnostic docs on the "vendor docs must be authoritative / 100% correct" norm; "incorrect documentation is
  worse than no documentation." Dorc's AI-mass-authorship is uniquely permitted, but this is the exact norm it
  must clear. [B-clang-diag-docs-rfc-2024], echoed by [A-writethedocs-principles-2024] "Current".
- fin-error-surface-poles: rustc = per-code standalone markdown, doctested, NO cross-page dedup (pole a);
  Elm = terminal error links OUT to 16 standalone per-CONCEPT hint files (pole b); staticcheck = generated
  per-code standalone pages clustered in families. None occupy the inline-transclusion middle.
  [B-rustc-dev-guide-error-codes-2026], [B-elm-hints-comparing-custom-types-2019], [C-staticcheck-checks-index-2026].

### goal-2 — techcomm single-sourcing

> [A-baker-frankenbooks-must-die-2012]:whole (relevance: +1:SURE)
> distinguishes topic-as-information-design vs topic-as-COMPOSITIONAL-mechanism; Frankenbook = compose from
> fragments without info-design discipline: "all the pieces extracted from an original set of books have
> been threaded together into one monstrous info-glob... no page is page one."
> DESIGN LEVER: "One of the key design goals of the SPFE architecture is to make sure that Frankenbooks are
> NOT the path of least resistance... It is really easy to create a Frankenbook with a map; it is really
> difficult to create one with a query." (the compose MECHANISM shapes the outcome — steer least-resistance
> toward coherence.)

> [A-baker-reuse-tactic-not-strategy-2014]:whole (relevance: +1:SURE)
> "there is a big difference between 'truth' and 'text'. If you create a single source of text and call it a
> single source of truth, you are effectively saying there can only be one expression of any truth."
> DISCONFIRMING NUANCE (the blessed case): "reusing text where you would have been writing substantially
> the same text anyway is clearly the right thing to do... If you are producing multiple variants of the same
> basic product... reusing common material between them only makes sense." (Dorc's sibling error codes
> sharing a concept paragraph = exactly "substantially the same text anyway".)
> "producing your help system from the same files as your manual does not actually produce a very good help
> system... the result is often a Frankenbook." (reuse across DIFFERENT rhetorical purposes/media fails.)

> [B-baker-chunking-translation-2011]:whole (relevance: +1:SURE)
> "fine chunking tends to obscure context, making the content impossible to translate reliably... the source
> may be reused in new contexts later without the translator being involved again or ever seeing the content
> in its new context." (authoring/using-out-of-context, decorrelated to translation.)

> [A-writethedocs-principles-2024]:§ARID / §Unique / §Current (relevance: +1:SURE)
> "if you adhere strictly to this DRY principle when writing documentation, you won't get far... ARID does
> not mean WET... keep things as DRY as possible but also recognize that you'll inevitably need some amount
> of 'moisture'."
> §Unique: "Eliminate content overlap between separate SOURCES... prevent any parallel maintenance... of the
> same information across multiple sources." (single-source; repetition-in-output ≠ overlap-in-source.)
> §Current: "Consider incorrect documentation to be worse than missing documentation." (authoritative norm.)

> [A-google-style-cross-references-2025]:§Provide-context-on-the-page / §Choose-links-selectively (relevance: +1:SURE)
> "When possible, provide help in context rather than linking elsewhere... Define a term. Briefly explain a
> concept. Provide a couple of steps."
> "Each link creates a decision for the reader, adding cognitive load. Each link is also a chance for the
> reader to leave the page and lose their place."
> "if you need readers to understand another product's software or standards, it's better to link... But if a
> few sentences of basic information is all your readers need, then it's better to provide that context and
> save your readers the trip." (inline own-domain; link others'-domain.)

> [B-tomjohnson-single-sourcing-over-2025]:whole (relevance: +1:SURE)

### goal-4 — decorrelated seam evidence (legal assembly; translation memory)

> "Encrustation... introduce[s] essentially random variations... stripped of any context." error-correction

### goal-5 — authorship-in-situ (woven; where edit happens vs where reader reads)

- edit-through-transclusion EXISTS natively: Wikipedia composite pages let you edit a transcluded section from
  the composite view; saved back to source. [B-wikipedia-transclusion-help-2026]. But article-prose transclusion
  is discouraged in mainspace (harder to edit).
- the both-views architecture is a defended real pattern: [B-memoq-segmentation-2025].
- the consistent lesson: edit-in-a-vacuum (fragment without surroundings) is the root cause of incoherence
  [A-baker-frankenbooks-must-die-2012], [B-baker-chunking-translation-2011], [B-tm-friend-or-foe-deck-2018],
  [C-stilo-conref-limitations-2023]; and edit-in-composed-context is what Google/Henley implicitly endorse
  (one annotated, whole, in-context template; provide context on the page).

## Evidence AGAINST inline prose transclusion (disconfirming collection)

1. techcomm's rotted middle IS mid-size shared prose paragraphs; the surviving granularities are the extremes
   (whole-file OR context-free atom). [B-tomjohnson-single-sourcing-over-2025], [C-stilo-conref-limitations-2023],
   [B-mdbook-includes-2024].
2. Wikipedia, with full transclusion machinery available, DELIBERATELY chose summarize-and-link + accepted
   DUPLICATION as the article-prose default; transclusion of prose is the cautious exception, and mainspace
   norm discourages storing article text in templates. [B-wikipedia-summary-style-2026],
   [B-wikipedia-transclusion-help-2026].
3. the semantic seam: a reused fragment can silently lose fit with its host, and the error-correcting feedback
   that catches this in normal writing DOESN'T fire for by-rote reuse. [A-blackhole-boilerplate-choi-gulati-scott-2017].
4. parameterized prose has a hard complexity ceiling: Wikipedia's arc ended in a Lua priesthood off the
   authorable surface; legal templates "collapse under their own weight" with nested conditionals.
   [B-wikipedia-lua-2026], [B-automationlogs-common-mistakes-2026].
5. AI-mass-authored explain pages face an authoritative-correctness wall that a shared-inline model amplifies
   (one wrong shared paragraph is wrong on every page). [B-clang-diag-docs-rfc-2024], [A-writethedocs-principles-2024].
6. legal interpretive norm = read-as-a-coherent-whole; seams introduced at adaptation/graft points cause real,
   litigated harm. [B-yieldpoint-frankenstein-slip-2024].
